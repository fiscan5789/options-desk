How this reads the tape · scan log
Every 5-minute bar of the regular session up to the as-of time is classified (trend bar, doji, strength, close location) against the 20-bar EMA and the 20-day average daily range (ADR). The engine tracks the always-in direction (a flip needs a spike, meaning two consecutive trend bars or one large trend bar breaking recent bars, and a follow-through bar without an opposite close; a slower flip happens after 4 closes on the wrong side of a turning EMA), scores the current leg on the signs of strength in Trends ch.19, scores each day type (trend from the open, small pullback trend, spike and channel, trending trading range, trading range, reversal, trend resumption, stairs), and maps the time of day to Brooks's three periods. The bias score (−100 to +100) combines always-in, leg strength, day type, EMA position, location in the day's range, gap behavior and the daily chart, then gets damped for trading-range conditions and exhaustion. Brooks's probabilities (50/55/60/70%) are his rules of thumb. The engine history figures come from replaying this same engine every 30 minutes across the loaded sessions for each ETF and checking what happened after each call. Volume is shown as relative volume for this time of day; Brooks treats it as secondary, so it only nudges exhaustion and context. Market data is 15 minutes delayed on Polygon's Starter plan, so a live scan reads bars up to about 15 minutes old. The bar still forming is always dropped. The optional live price box compares a price you type from a real-time quote with the last delayed bar. CURRENT means price is within 10% of ADR of the last bar. AGING means 10–25%, or the score at the live price falls into the two-sided band. STALE means 25% or more, or price crossed the opening range, EMA, yesterday's high/low/close or the start of the channel against the bias. Only location and EMA position are re-scored at the live price. A new scan clears the box.
