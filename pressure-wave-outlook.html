How this builds the outlook · scan log
Each 5-minute bar of the regular session up to the as-of time is read the way the two books read a chart. From Bob Volman's Understanding Price Action: the dominant pressure (slope of the 25 EMA and which side the closes sit on), buildup (tight clusters pressing against a barrier), the quality of the latest break (proper, tease or premature), false highs and lows, round-number magnets, and his list of conditions for skipping a trade. From Schlotmann and Czubatinski's Technical Analysis Masterclass: the chart phase (trend, correction, consolidation, breakout, reversal), price waves (length, angle, impulse-to-correction ratio, higher highs and lows), candle character and patterns, support/resistance and supply/demand zones with order absorption, and one indicator per group (RSI, MACD, Bollinger Bands at 2.5 SD), plus the 60-minute and daily trend. Volman's EUR/USD numbers are scaled to each ETF's 20-day average daily range (ADR): his 20-pip target is 0.2 ADR, the 10-pip stop is 0.1 ADR, the 14-pip minimum room is 0.14 ADR, and round numbers are spaced at about half an ADR. Waves are built with a zigzag that needs a reversal of at least 9% of ADR (or 2.5 normal bars). The score adds the eleven factors (−100 to +100). The grade counts failed conditions: A = none, B = 1, C = 2, Skip = 3 or more. Neither book uses volume beyond noting that breaks need participation, so relative volume is shown for context and is not scored. The projected range uses this ETF's own history and is not from either book. The engine history replays this same engine every 30 minutes on every loaded session using only bars up to that time. It checks whether the close went the outlook's way and whether a Volman bracket (+0.2 ADR target before a −0.1 ADR stop) would have won. That bracket breaks even at 33%.
